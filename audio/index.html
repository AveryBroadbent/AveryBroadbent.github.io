Audio Design Showcase

While not formerly trained through any specific institution, audio remains one of my biggest and most passionate skillsets in my professional career. An upbringing of highschool performance art combined with a love of detail gave me a well trained ear for the experience of sound, which in my years at university turned into a skillset I had a knack for in game development.

Each audio file provided is the same used in the final project they were made for.

28 Leicester Square

(August 2025 - April 2026)

I handled and organized everything to do with sound in this project. That included:

  • Production spreadsheet of every needed sfx (approved by our project lead)
  • Designs of the sounds themselves
  • Recording, Sampling, & foley work
  • Audio balancing both out and out of engine
  • Audio implementation (+ variation & pitch shifting)
  • Custom audio system framework (Engineering showcase)
  • Music composition and production (Music showcase)

This project was the first to truly let me practice audio as a sole focus, instead of it being relegated to a skillset I had to leave on the backburner until the project "called for it" (which was often just moments before the final deadline). As such, the project stands as the single best reference point for my audio design skillset.

Out of everything in the project, around 60-70% of the original sound samples were either recorded or digitally generated by me. The rest were pulled from existing samples found on freesound.org.

By the end of the project, I had made around 80+ finalized sound effects for the final game.

-Ambience-

The two ambience tracks are where the game gets most of its atmosphere. The first one here is the general ambience, an 8 minute loop that plays regardless of position. Interspersed are random bits of foley mostly from a general soundpack I had access to, along side some occasional drone samples I had lying around that are themselves unnerving to listen to.

LS-GeneralAmbience.ogg

If you stand by the windows, you can hear the outdoor ambience, which done by positionally moving the audio player around to the closest window in 3D space. Combining this with the general ambience in-game leads to some nice layering, I think.

LS-WindowAMB.ogg

As a bonus, at the start of the game is a very important table that the designers struggled to properly get playtesters to notice. So, I thought it would be fun to have the radio on that table play some old music from around the time period. Even if it's a really minor part of the final project, it was a fun easter egg that I spent a decent amount of time researching and hunting for; The following is a nearly century year old recording of Love's Old Sweet song, public domain, filtered through an EQ I balanced to properly sell the effect of the radio.

LovesOSSradio

-Doors-

Each door had 2 opening samples, alongside 2 closing samples, allowing for some light variation. I recorded the samples myself, taking advantage of the fact that my apartment building was legally a historical building, and consequentially hundreds of years old, giving me plenty of wiggle room to emphasis how 'not modern' the doors were.

DoorOpen1.wav

DoorOpen2.wav

DoorClose1.wav

DoorClose2.wav

Additionally I made samples for struggling against a door when using wrong items on them, and a key unlocking sample for when using a correct key.

DoorFail.wav

UnlockDoor.wav

-UI-

I mostly relied on pitch shifting to give these samples variation. Inventory sounds were made via a simple synth in my DAW.

InventoryIn.wav

InventoryOut.wav

Menu sounds I made by layering samples of myself taking sharp breaths over each other and cranking up the reverb + some light EQ balancing to emphasis the low frequencies. Pulling a little bit from darksouls with this one.

MenuIn.wav

MenuOut.wav

-Foley interactions-

The game revolves around finding 3 particular objects to open the lab. As such, I felt it was important that each of those objects had a distinct sound for picking up and placing them. Each were made from foley samples I recorded.

SaltPickup.wav

PlantInteract.wav

BottleInteract.wav

-Monster sfx-

I ended up making a total of 23 unique sfx for the monsters, including variations. Each sample was sourced by friends both on & off the project making unholy noises into their microphones for me. Not displayed here due to sheer quantity, but you can hear them in the final game.

-Puzzle Sfx-

Puzzle solve and Puzzle Fail jingles, respectively. These were just made with a piano midi to echo the main motif.

LS-PuzzleComplete.wav

LS-PuzzleFailure.wav

We needed a way to signify when a door was unlocked by a puzzle's completion, so using my ancient apartment doors again I somehow edited this gear sound together. I think I recorded myself twisting a doorknob, and then turned that into a midi instrument? Something like that.

GearsUnlock.wav

SPIN-MECH FURY

(July 2026)
